## The separation this file must not violate

**Nothing in this file is evidence about any audio product, and no product record is evidence about these documents.**

These are public standards published by the ITU and the EBU. They describe what a conforming measurement is. They say nothing about what iZotope, FabFilter, or any other vendor actually implements, and this repository holds no evidence that any of them implements any of it.

`GAP-OZONE-0001` — that the inspected Ozone 12 pages never name ITU-R BS.1770 — **remains true and unaffected by this file.** So does `GAP-OZONE-0002`. Ozone's meter scales are labelled "EBU +9" and "EBU +18", and `OBS-T3341-013` below records that EBU Tech 3341 §2.7 defines scales by those names; those two facts are **not** a bridge. A vendor using a standard's name in a UI label establishes nothing about the vendor's algorithm. Any Spectre document that treats the coincidence of names as evidence has fabricated a claim.

Likewise, `GAP-OZONE-0005` — that no streaming-platform target value appears anywhere in the Ozone corpus — is now joined by `GAP-LOUDNESS-0003`: no streaming-platform target value appears anywhere in these standards either. The −23.0 LUFS figure in `OBS-R128-002` is a **broadcast** target and must never be presented as a streaming target.

## ITU-R BS.1770-5 — scope and algorithm structure

- `OBS-BS1770-001` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, "recommends" clauses 1–5): The Recommendation partitions its scope by annex. Annex 1 applies to programmes of up to five main channels per ITU-R BS.775 (mono, stereo, 3/2 multichannel). Annex 3 applies to larger channel counts such as the configurations of ITU-R BS.2051. Annex 4 applies to object-based audio and to combinations of channel- and object-based audio. Annex 2 supplies **guidelines** for true-peak indication, and clause 5 explicitly permits "a method that gives similar or superior results" instead.
- `OBS-BS1770-002` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, opening summary): The algorithm has four stages, in order — "K" frequency weighting; mean-square calculation for each channel; channel-weighted summation, in which surround channels carry larger weights and the LFE channel is excluded; and gating of 400 ms blocks overlapping by 75%, using two thresholds, the first at −70 LKFS and the second at −10 dB relative to the level measured after the first threshold is applied.
- `OBS-BS1770-003` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, footnote 1 and the text following it): K-weighting is a cascade of exactly two second-order filter stages. Stage 1 is a shelving filter that accounts for the acoustic effects of the head, modelled as a rigid sphere. Stage 2 is a high-pass filter, identified in the text after equation (7) as the RLB (revised low-frequency B-curve) weighting. Both stages use the same second-order signal-flow structure.
- `OBS-BS1770-026` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, closing paragraph, and "recommends" NOTE 1): The algorithm is stated to be effective on audio programmes typical of broadcast content but **is not, in general, suitable for estimating the subjective loudness of pure tones**. NOTE 1 to the recommends clauses states that measured loudness is an estimation of subjective loudness involving some degree of uncertainty depending on listeners, audio material, and listening conditions.

## ITU-R BS.1770-5 — K-weighting coefficients

- `OBS-BS1770-004` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, Table 1): The stage 1 pre-filter coefficients are `b0 = 1.53512485958697`, `b1 = −2.69169618940638`, `b2 = 1.19839281085285`, `a1 = −1.69065929318241`, `a2 = 0.73248077421585`.
- `OBS-BS1770-005` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, Table 2): The stage 2 weighting-curve coefficients are `b0 = 1.0`, `b1 = −2.0`, `b2 = 1.0`, `a1 = −1.99004745483398`, `a2 = 0.99007225036621`. The numerator `1, −2, 1` is the canonical second-order high-pass numerator.
- `OBS-BS1770-006` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, paragraphs following Table 1 and Table 2): **Both coefficient sets are stated to be for a sampling rate of 48 kHz.** The text states that implementations at other sampling rates will require different coefficient values, which "should be chosen to provide the same frequency response that the specified filter provides at 48 kHz". It further states that coefficient values may need to be quantized due to available hardware precision, and that tests have shown the algorithm's performance is not sensitive to small variations in these coefficients.
- `OBS-BS1770-007` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: `OBS-BS1770-006` is the single most consequential constraint in this file for Spectre. A DAW runs at whatever rate the audio device is set to, and the standard supplies coefficients for exactly one rate while stating a *requirement on the result* (same frequency response) rather than a procedure for reaching it. Spectre would have to choose between deriving coefficients per rate by a documented method, or resampling to 48 kHz before measuring — and either choice is a Spectre engineering decision that the standard does not make, requiring its own rationale row. See `GAP-LOUDNESS-0001`.

## ITU-R BS.1770-5 — mean square, channel weighting, and units

- `OBS-BS1770-008` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, equation (1)): The power `z_i` for channel `i` is the mean square of the pre-filtered signal `y_i` over the measurement interval `T` — the time integral of `y_i²` over `T`, divided by `T`. The channel set is `I = {L, R, C, Ls, Rs}`. The input to this stage is the signal **after** both pre-filter stages.
- `OBS-BS1770-009` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, equation (2)): Loudness is `L_K = −0.691 + 10·log10( Σ_i G_i · z_i )`, expressed in LKFS, where `G_i` are the per-channel weighting coefficients.
- `OBS-BS1770-010` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, Table 3): The per-channel weighting coefficients are — Left `G_L = 1.0 (0 dB)`; Right `G_R = 1.0 (0 dB)`; Centre `G_C = 1.0 (0 dB)`; Left surround `G_Ls = 1.41 (~ +1.5 dB)`; Right surround `G_Rs = 1.41 (~ +1.5 dB)`. The LFE channel is not included in the measurement (Annex 1 opening summary and the text accompanying Figure 1). For a programme with fewer than five channels, some inputs are simply unused.
- `OBS-BS1770-011` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, NOTE 1 and NOTE 2 following equation (7)): The constant `−0.691` in equation (2) exists to cancel out the K-weighting gain at 997 Hz. A 0 dBFS, 997 Hz sine wave applied to the left, centre, or right channel input yields an indicated loudness of exactly **−3.01 LKFS**. NOTE 2 records that IEC 61606 specifies 997 Hz as the reference measurement frequency, which may be stated in non-critical contexts as the nominal 1 kHz.
- `OBS-BS1770-012` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: `OBS-BS1770-011`'s −3.01 LKFS figure is a self-contained unit test. A single full-scale 997 Hz sine into one of L/C/R must read −3.01 LKFS; this exercises both filter stages, the mean square, the channel weighting, and the offset constant in one assertion, with no test-signal download required. It is proposed as the first test any Spectre loudness implementation should pass, not as a conformance claim.
- `OBS-BS1770-013` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, paragraph following equation (7)): The designation **LKFS** signifies "Loudness, K-weighted, relative to nominal full scale". The unit is stated to be equivalent to a decibel, in that an increase in signal level of 1 dB causes the loudness reading to increase by 1 LKFS.

## ITU-R BS.1770-5 — gating

- `OBS-BS1770-014` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, paragraph preceding equation (3)): A gating block is a set of contiguous audio samples of duration `T_g = 400 ms`, "to the nearest sample". The overlap of each gating block **shall** be 75% of the gating block duration. The measurement interval **shall** be constrained such that it ends at the end of a gating block, and incomplete gating blocks at the end of the interval are not used.
- `OBS-BS1770-015` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, equation (3)): The `j`-th gating block of channel `i` is the mean square over the window starting at `T_g · j · step`, where `step = 1 − overlap`. With the 75% overlap that `OBS-BS1770-014` makes mandatory, `step = 0.25` and the block hop is `400 ms × 0.25 = 100 ms`. The block index runs `j = 0, 1, 2, …, floor((T − T_g) / (T_g · step))`.
- `OBS-BS1770-016` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, equation (4)): The `j`-th gating block loudness is `l_j = −0.691 + 10·log10( Σ_i G_i · z_ij )` — the same functional form as equation (2), evaluated per block. The offset constant and channel weights are identical.
- `OBS-BS1770-017` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, equations (5)–(7) and the paragraph introducing them): Gating is a **two-stage process, absolute first, then relative**. The absolute threshold is `Γ_a = −70 LKFS`. Gating blocks below the absolute threshold are not used in the relative gating calculation. The relative threshold `Γ_r` is computed by measuring the gated loudness using only the blocks that passed the absolute threshold, and subtracting **10** from that result. The final gated loudness `L_KG = −0.691 + 10·log10( Σ_i G_i · (1/|J_g|) · Σ_{j∈J_g} z_ij )`, where per equation (7) `J_g = { j : l_j > Γ_r and l_j > Γ_a }` — both conditions, not the relative one alone.
- `OBS-BS1770-018` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: The gated loudness in equation (5)/(7) averages the **per-block mean-square power** `z_ij` across the surviving blocks and takes the logarithm once, at the end. It does **not** average the per-block loudness values `l_j` in the log domain. Those are different numbers. An implementation that retains only `l_j` in dB and averages those will be wrong; it must retain per-channel power. Recorded as a candidate because it is a reading of the equation's structure, not a sentence the standard writes.

## ITU-R BS.1770-5 — true-peak measurement (Annex 2)

- `OBS-BS1770-019` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 2, opening paragraph): True-peak level is defined as the maximum positive or negative value of the signal waveform **in the continuous time domain**, and this value may be higher than the largest sample value in the time-sampled domain. Annex 2 describes estimation within a **single channel** linear PCM digital audio signal, and its discussion presumes a 48 kHz sample rate.
- `OBS-BS1770-020` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 2, §1 Summary): The processing stages, in order, are — (1) attenuate by 12.04 dB; (2) 4× over-sampling; (3) low-pass filter; (4) take the absolute value; (5) convert to dB TP.
- `OBS-BS1770-021` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 2, §3): The 12.04 dB attenuation is a 2-bit shift whose purpose is to provide headroom for subsequent processing that could use integer arithmetic. **This step is stated to be unnecessary if the calculations are performed in floating point.** It must afterwards be compensated by amplifying by 12.04 dB (a 2-bit left shift), normalising overall gain to unity; the text notes this generally requires a numeric format able to represent values above the original format's full-scale range, which floating point satisfies, and that calibrating the meter scale is an alternative to amplifying the result.
- `OBS-BS1770-022` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 2, §3): The 4× over-sampling filter raises the sampling rate from 48 kHz to 192 kHz. The text states that **higher sampling rates and over-sampling ratios are preferred**, and that incoming signals at higher sampling rates require proportionately less over-sampling — giving the explicit example that for a 96 kHz input, 2× over-sampling would be sufficient.
- `OBS-BS1770-023` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 2, §3, coefficient table): The Recommendation supplies **one** set of filter coefficients that "would satisfy the requirements", identified as an **order-48, 4-phase, FIR interpolating** filter — that is, 4 polyphase branches of 12 taps each, 48 coefficients total, printed as a 4-column by 12-row table. The table itself is not reproduced here; an implementer reads it from Annex 2. Two structural properties were verified against the printed values and are recorded as retrieval anchors: every coefficient is an exact multiple of 2⁻¹⁶ (each value times 65536 is an integer), and the largest-magnitude tap, `0.9721679687500`, appears once in every phase. The wording "one set … that would satisfy the requirements" makes this an example that meets the requirement, not the sole mandated filter.
- `OBS-BS1770-024` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 2, §3, final paragraph): Meters that follow these guidelines **and that use an over-sampled sampling rate of at least 192 kHz** should indicate the result in units of dB TP, computed as `20·log10` of the attenuated, over-sampled, filtered, absolute value, then adding 12.04 dB. The designation "dB TP" signifies decibels relative to 100% full scale, true-peak measurement.
- `OBS-BS1770-025` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Attachment 1 to Annex 2, informative): The maximum under-read of an over-sampled peak meter is derived as `20·log(cos(π · f_norm / n))` for over-sampling ratio `n` and maximum normalized frequency `f_norm`, on the reasoning that a peak can be missed by at most half the over-sampled sampling period. The tabulated maxima are — ratio 4: `0.554 dB` at `f_norm = 0.45`, `0.688 dB` at `f_norm = 0.5`; ratio 8: `0.136` / `0.169`; ratio 10: `0.087` / `0.108`; ratio 12: `0.060` / `0.075`; ratio 14: `0.044` / `0.055`; ratio 16: `0.034` / `0.042`; ratio 32: `0.008` / `0.010`. **This attachment is explicitly informative**, and the ITU footnote records that the text was contributed by AES Standards Working Group SC-02-01.
- `OBS-BS1770-027` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Attachment 1 to Annex 2, informative): Over-sampling is described as inserting zero-value samples between the original samples and then applying a low-pass interpolation filter, after which the ordinary peak-sample algorithm is run on the over-sampled signal. The attachment notes that because the over-sampler's output is never listened to, passband-ripple and word-length precision requirements are lower than for an audio-path sample-rate converter, which considerably reduces the required tap count. It also notes that DC exclusion, if wanted, can be had with a low-order IIR high-pass at the meter input — **but that if the purpose is overload elimination, DC content must be maintained and metered.**
- `OBS-BS1770-028` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: Reading `OBS-BS1770-025` against `OBS-T3341-018` produces a concrete engineering consequence. The EBU's true-peak minimum-requirement tolerance is `+0.2 / −0.4 dBTP`, while the informative under-read table gives 4× over-sampling a worst-case under-read of `0.554–0.688 dB` — larger than the permitted `−0.4 dB` — and gives 8× an under-read of `0.136–0.169 dB`, comfortably inside it. This suggests that a Spectre true-peak meter at 48 kHz should use **at least 8×**, not the 4× of Annex 2's worked example. It is an inference across two documents, not a statement either one makes, and it would need its own rationale row before any Spectre spec asserts a factor.

## ITU-R BS.1770-5 — advanced and object-based configurations (Annexes 3 and 4)

- `OBS-BS1770-029` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 3, §1 and Table 4): The extension for the loudspeaker configurations of ITU-R BS.2051 changes **only the third stage**. The first, second, and fourth stages — filtering and the gating procedure — are stated to be the same as in Annex 1 and independent of channel position. The number of input channels `N` excludes the LFE channels. The weighting `G_i` becomes position-dependent on azimuth `θ` and elevation `φ`: for `|φ| < 30°`, `G_i = 1.00 (±0 dB)` when `|θ| < 60°`, `G_i = 1.41 (+1.5 dB)` when `60° ≤ |θ| ≤ 120°`, and `G_i = 1.00 (±0 dB)` when `120° < |θ| ≤ 180°`; for every other elevation, `G_i = 1.00 (±0 dB)`.
- `OBS-BS1770-030` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 3, Table 5): Table 5 resolves Table 4 into explicit per-channel weightings for the ten BS.2051 loudspeaker configurations labelled A through J (0+2+0 through 4+7+0), by loudspeaker label. The 1.41 weighting attaches to the M+060/M−060, M+090/M−090, and M+110/M−110 positions; every other listed position, including all upper (U), top (T), and bottom (B) layer positions, carries 1.00.
- `OBS-BS1770-031` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 4): Object-based audio signals, or combinations of channel- and object-based signals, are **first rendered to a specific loudspeaker configuration**, and the objective loudness is then measured from the rendered output using the algorithms of Annex 1 and Annex 3. The Annex states that objective loudness may differ depending on rendering conditions, and that the loudspeaker configuration and rendering algorithm used in the measurement **should be reported**.
- `OBS-BS1770-032` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: `OBS-BS1770-029`'s structure — filtering and gating fixed, channel weighting swapped per layout — is the natural decomposition for an implementation too. A Spectre meter that separated "the K-weighting and gating engine" from "a channel-weight table selected by bus layout" would follow the standard's own factoring and could extend from stereo to surround without touching the gating path. This is an architectural observation, not an adopted design.

## EBU R 128 — the delivery target

- `OBS-R128-001` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, Document History table): R 128 was first published February 2010. V2 (August 2011) changed the relative gate from −8 to −10 LU. V3 (June 2014) changed the Target Level tolerance to ±0.5 LU except for live programmes. V4 (August 2020) updated tolerances and added references to R 128 s1 and s2. V5 (November 2023) added references to R 128 s3, s4, and Tech 3401. The document read here is **V5, dated November 2023**, footered "EBU R 128-2023".
- `OBS-R128-002` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "recommends" clauses g and h): Programme Loudness shall generally be used to normalise an audio signal, and **the Programme Loudness Level shall be normalised to a Target Level of −23.0 LUFS**. Where attaining the Target Level is not practically achievable — the document's own example is live programmes — **a tolerance of ±1.0 LU is permitted**. The clause adds that a broadcaster should ensure deviation towards the limits of the tolerance does not become standard practice.
- `OBS-R128-003` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "recommends" clause i): A **separate** tolerance of **±0.2 LU** is allowed for the implementation of loudness workflows — the document's example is Quality Control environments — in order to account for measurement errors. This is a distinct allowance from the ±1.0 LU of `OBS-R128-002` and applies to a different thing: measurement error, not production practicality.
- `OBS-R128-004` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "recommends" clause j): In special cases the Programme Loudness Level **may** deliberately be normalised to a Target Level lower than −23.0 LUFS. Such an exception shall be clearly indicated, to ensure the lower level is not compensated downstream.
- `OBS-R128-005` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "recommends" clause k): The measurement **shall** be made with a loudness meter compliant with ITU-R BS.1770 — explicitly "including the level-gating method described in equation (7)" — **and** with EBU Tech 3341. This clause is the normative bridge that makes `OBS-BS1770-017`'s two-stage gating and the Tech 3341 meter specification binding for R 128 compliance.
- `OBS-R128-006` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "recommends" clause l): The audio signal shall generally be measured **in its entirety**, without emphasis on specific foreground elements such as speech, music, or sound effects. R 128 does not use a speech-gated or dialogue-anchored measurement.
- `OBS-R128-007` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "recommends" clause m): **The True Peak Level of a programme shall not exceed −1 dBTP during production (linear audio)**, measured with a meter compliant with ITU-R BS.1770 and EBU Tech 3341. The measurement tolerance is **±0.3 dB**, stated for signals with a bandwidth limited to 20 kHz. The clause explicitly adds that Permitted Maximum True Peak Levels **may be lower** for different distribution systems and data-reduction rates, directing the reader to EBU Tech 3344.
- `OBS-R128-008` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "considering" clause e and its footnote 1): ITU-R BS.1770 introduced the measures LU (Loudness Unit) and LUFS (Loudness Units, referenced to Full Scale). Footnote 1 states directly that **"LUFS" is equivalent to "LKFS"** (the designation BS.1770 uses), and that the EBU uses LUFS as compliant with international naming conventions. Read with `OBS-BS1770-013` and `OBS-T3341-010`, this fixes the full unit relationship: LUFS ≡ LKFS, absolute; LU is the relative unit; and 1 LU = 1 dB.
- `OBS-R128-009` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "considering" clause f): The level-gated measurement of Programme Loudness defined in BS.1770 equation (7) is characterised as measuring **foreground loudness**, and is stated to be advantageous for improving the loudness matching of programmes with a wide loudness range. This is the stated rationale for gating at all.
- `OBS-R128-010` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, Definitions): Programme Loudness is the **integrated** loudness over the duration of a programme; Programme Loudness Level is its value in LUFS. Loudness Range (LRA) is "the distribution of loudness within a programme". Maximum True Peak Level is "the maximum value of the audio signal waveform of a programme in the continuous time domain". A "Programme" is an individual self-contained item, and short-form content — adverts, trailers, promos, interstitials — is explicitly to be considered a programme in this context.
- `OBS-R128-011` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "further recommends" clauses n and o): LRA measured per EBU Tech 3342 **may** be used to evaluate a programme's loudness variation, its potential subsequent dynamic treatment, and the dynamic integrity of a distribution path. Maximum Momentary Loudness and Maximum Short-term Loudness measured per Tech 3341 **may** be used to determine whether a programme exceeds the upper loudness tolerance limit of the target audience. Both are permissive "may" clauses, unlike the "shall" of `OBS-R128-002` and `OBS-R128-007`.
- `OBS-R128-012` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, footnote 2 to the Definitions section): **For programmes shorter than 1 minute, the use of the Loudness Range measure is not recommended**, due to too few data points — the footnote states that LRA is based on Short-term Loudness values using a 3-second window.
- `OBS-R128-013` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, "recommends" clauses p–v and References): Loudness Metadata shall correctly indicate the actual Programme Loudness. R 128 defers whole categories of guidance to documents **not retrieved for this ledger**: short-form content to R 128 s1; streaming to R 128 s2; radio to R 128 s3 and Tech 3401; cinematic content to R 128 s4; production practice to Tech 3343; distribution and reproduction to Tech 3344.
- `OBS-R128-014` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: R 128 is a **broadcast** recommendation authored by a broadcasting union, and every target and limit in it is scoped to broadcast programme production and exchange. It is the only delivery target now citable from this ledger, which creates an obvious temptation to reuse −23.0 LUFS and −1 dBTP as though they were general mastering targets. They are not, and R 128 itself routes streaming to a separate supplement (`OBS-R128-013`) that this repository has not read. Whether Spectre surfaces any target at all is an open product decision.

## EBU Tech 3341 — "EBU Mode" metering

- `OBS-T3341-001` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2): "EBU Mode" is an **activatable meter mode**, not a property of a product. When EBU Mode is activated, the meter shall comply with Tech 3341 and with the underlying ITU and EBU recommendations except where differences are explicitly required. A meter **may** provide alternatives to any or all of the EBU Mode specifications, but when such alternatives are selected **the meter is no longer in EBU Mode**. The specification explicitly does not concern graphical/UI details or the implementation of a meter.
- `OBS-T3341-002` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.1): Three time scales are defined — the shortest is **Momentary**, abbreviated `M`; the intermediate is **Short-term**, abbreviated `S`; the programme- or segment-wise scale is **Integrated**, abbreviated `I`. In an EBU Mode "live meter" all three shall be available, though not necessarily displayed at the same time. A non-live meter (the document's example is a file-based software meter) that implements only a subset of the time scales **is still considered compliant** if that subset complies. A footnote defines a "live meter" as one usable in a live environment, measuring audio as it happens, and prefers that term over "real-time meter".
- `OBS-T3341-003` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.1): The meter shall be able to display the **maximum** value of Momentary Loudness and of Short-term Loudness, and these maximum values are reset when the Integrated Loudness measurement is reset.
- `OBS-T3341-004` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.2): The EBU Mode measurement parameters are — **Momentary** uses a sliding **rectangular** time window of length **0.4 s** and **is not gated**; **Short-term** uses a sliding **rectangular** window of length **3 s**, **is not gated**, and for live meters the update rate shall be **at least 10 Hz**; **Integrated** uses the gating described in ITU-R BS.1770, and for live meters its update rate shall be **at least 1 Hz**. §2.2 opens by stating that in all cases the measurement is performed as specified in ITU-R BS.1770.
- `OBS-T3341-005` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.2): **Further slowdown of the attack or release (decay) parts of the loudness signals, after the sliding rectangular time windows, shall not be employed in EBU Mode.** The bracketed note records that ITU-R BS.1771-1 prescribes a 1st-order IIR filter with a 0.4 s time constant for Momentary Loudness, that the EBU has received indications a smaller time constant might be preferable with the IIR method, and that the original rectangular-window definition therefore remains in EBU Mode until evidence clarifies the issue.
- `OBS-T3341-006` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.2): Other window lengths or time constants **are allowed** in a loudness meter offering EBU Mode, but it should be clearly indicated on the meter whether or not the EBU parameter set is in effect.
- `OBS-T3341-007` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.2): An EBU Mode meter shall at least let the user (1) start/pause/continue the measurement of Integrated Loudness **and** Loudness Range **simultaneously** — switching between "running" and "stand-by" — and (2) reset Integrated Loudness and Loudness Range **simultaneously**, regardless of which state the meter is in. The two measures are specified as a single user-controlled unit.
- `OBS-T3341-008` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.3): The Integrated Loudness gating is restated as — an absolute "silence" gating threshold at **−70 LUFS** used to compute the absolute-gated loudness level; a relative gating threshold **10 LU below** that absolute-gated level; and application of the threshold to the loudness of **400 ms blocks with a constant overlap between consecutive gating blocks of 75%**. If the end of an integrated measurement falls inside a gating block, **the incomplete gating block shall be discarded.** These figures agree exactly with `OBS-BS1770-014` and `OBS-BS1770-017`, stated in LUFS rather than LKFS.
- `OBS-T3341-009` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.3, Note): Because the relative threshold is itself derived from a loudness measurement, the Note states that **in a live meter the integrated loudness has to be recalculated from the preceding stored loudness levels of the blocks, from the time the measurement was started** — recalculating the threshold, then applying it to the stored values — **every time the meter reading is updated**.
- `OBS-T3341-010` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.4): Loudness Range is abbreviated **LRA** and is measured in units of **LU**, and the section states that **1 LU is equivalent to 1 dB**. An EBU Mode meter shall be able to compute LRA for the audio signal corresponding to the integrated loudness measurement; the LRA computation is reset when Integrated is reset; the meter **may** be able to toggle the LRA display. **During the first 60 s of LRA measurement the meter shall indicate that the displayed LRA value is not yet to be considered stable**, by means the implementation may determine. The definition and reference implementation are delegated to EBU Tech 3342.
- `OBS-T3341-011` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.5): The naming convention is — a **relative** measurement (relative to a reference level, or a range) is written `L_K = xx.x LU`; an **absolute** measurement is written `L_K = xx.x LUFS`. The "L" indicates loudness level and the "K" indicates the frequency weighting used. The section frames this as an EBU proposal to resolve inconsistencies in ITU-R BS.1770 and BS.1771 and align with ISO and IEC conventions.
- `OBS-T3341-012` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.6): **In addition to** the specifications of ITU-R BS.1770 Annex 2, an EBU Mode meter's true-peak measure shall comply with the Table 1 tolerances for signals 15–23, intended as minimum-requirement tests. The **total** measurement error of the true-peak level must comply, **including any pass-band ripple in the upsampling filter and the "under-read"** described in BS.1770's Appendix 1 to Annex 2.
- `OBS-T3341-013` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.7): If a scale is shown, an EBU Mode meter shall offer **two** user-selectable scales — the **"EBU +9 scale"**, spanning **−18.0 LU to +9.0 LU (−41.0 LUFS to −14.0 LUFS)**, and the **"EBU +18 scale"**, spanning **−36.0 LU to +18.0 LU (−59.0 LUFS to −5.0 LUFS)**. The **EBU +9 scale shall be used by default.** The section notes this corresponds to the optional Requirement PLD-4 of ITU-R BS.1771. The display may alternatively be simply numerical.
- `OBS-T3341-014` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.7): The scale may be absolute in LUFS, or relative with the zero point mapped to another value such as the target loudness level, in which case the unit shall be LU. **For an EBU Mode meter the target loudness level shall be −23.0 LUFS = 0.0 LU**, as defined in EBU R 128. The meter shall offer both the relative and the absolute scale, and the location of the target/reference loudness level shall remain the same regardless of which is displayed.
- `OBS-T3341-015` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.8): The physical properties of the meter — size, colours, design — are not part of the specification. The required minimum feature set is that a compliant meter **shall be able to measure and display Programme Loudness, Loudness Range, and Maximum True Peak Level**, which for live meters need not be displayed at the same time. The meter **shall use a display precision of at most 1 decimal place** in all numerical loudness readouts. Integrated Loudness shall display in LU or LUFS, the unit switching with the scale, and **the unit shall be displayed for all values and scales at all times**. EBU Mode explicitly **does not specify** what the Integrated meter should indicate until there is sufficient input data for a valid result.
- `OBS-T3341-016` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.9, Calibration and alignment): For a basic calibration and alignment check, a **1 kHz stereo sine wave applied in phase to both channels with its peak level at −18 dBFS** is recommended, and **the meter should read −18.0 LUFS**. The section explicitly notes that BS.1770's stereo 1 kHz 0 dBFS example signal would be quite loud to listen to, and that since a given input attenuation produces the same reduction in the measured result, a quieter check signal is equivalent. A Note warns that **1 kHz lies on a filter slope within the algorithm**, making this calibration more critical than necessary with respect to both filter implementation accuracy and the accuracy of the tone's frequency, and that an error in the tone frequency can lead to a different result.
- `OBS-T3341-017` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.9, Table 1 cases 1–14): The loudness minimum-requirement tests hold the expected Integrated result at **−23.0 ±0.1 LUFS (0.0 ±0.1 LU)** across a battery of constructed signals. Case 1 is a 20 s stereo 1 kHz tone at −23.0 dBFS reading M, S, I = −23.0 ±0.1 LUFS; case 2 is the same at −33.0 dBFS reading −33.0 ±0.1 LUFS (−10.0 ±0.1 LU). Case 4 inserts 10 s segments at **−72.0 dBFS** and still expects I = −23.0 ±0.1 LUFS, exercising the −70 LUFS absolute gate. Case 5 uses 20 s at −26.0, 20.1 s at −20.0, and 20 s at −26.0 dBFS, exercising the relative gate. **Case 6 is a 5.0-channel test** with per-channel peak levels of −28.0 dBFS in L and R, −24.0 dBFS in C, and −30.0 dBFS in Ls and Rs, expecting I = −23.0 ±0.1 LUFS — which exercises the channel weighting of `OBS-BS1770-010`. Cases 9–14 pin Short-term and Momentary timing, including case 12's requirement that M read −23.0 ±0.1 LUFS and be **constant after 1 s** for a 0.18 s/0.22 s alternating pair, and case 9's requirement that S be constant after 3 s.
- `OBS-T3341-018` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341`, §2.9, Table 1 cases 15–19): The true-peak minimum-requirement tolerance is **+0.2 / −0.4 dBTP** — asymmetric, permitting twice as much under-read as over-read. Case 15 is a stereo sine at `fs/4` (12 kHz at a 48 kHz sample rate), amplitude 0.50 FFS, phase 0.0°, expecting max true-peak −6.0 dBTP; case 16 is the same at 45.0° phase; case 17 is `fs/6` at 60.0°; case 18 is `fs/8` at 67.5°; all four expect −6.0 +0.2/−0.4 dBTP. **Case 19 uses amplitude 1.41 FFS at `fs/4`, 45.0° phase, and expects a max true-peak level of +3.0 +0.2/−0.4 dBTP.** The synthesized tones should be tapered with a 10 ms fade-in and fade-out.
- `OBS-T3341-019` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: Case 19 (`OBS-T3341-018`) expects a true-peak reading **above 0 dBTP**. A true-peak meter must therefore represent and display values greater than full scale rather than clamping at 0 — which is also what `OBS-BS1770-021`'s note about needing a format that can represent values above the original full-scale range implies. Any Spectre true-peak readout that saturates at 0.0 would fail this case. Recorded as a candidate because it is a consequence of the test's expected value, not a sentence either document writes.
- `OBS-T3341-020` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: `OBS-T3341-009` is a hard architectural constraint, not a display note. Correct Integrated Loudness requires retaining the per-block loudness history for the entire measurement and re-deriving the relative threshold on every update — the measurement is not incrementally summarisable into fixed state. For a session-length measurement at the 100 ms block hop of `OBS-BS1770-015`, that is a monotonically growing array (36,000 entries per hour). Under `RT-001`/`RT-002` this cannot live on the audio thread: the callback can only publish per-block power through a bounded wait-free channel, with accumulation, thresholding, and re-derivation happening off-thread. Whether Spectre accepts unbounded growth, or bounds it with a histogram approximation and declares the deviation, is an open decision — and a histogram would be a **deviation from the specified algorithm** that must be stated as such rather than described as conformance.

## EBU Tech 3342 — Loudness Range (LRA)

- `OBS-T3342-001` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §2): Loudness Range, abbreviated **LRA**, quantifies the variation in a time-varying loudness measurement, on a **macroscopic** time-scale, in units of LU. Its computation is based on a measurement of loudness level as specified in ITU-R BS.1770 — **"albeit with a different relative gating threshold"**, the document's own parenthetical pointing to §3.1. The section explicitly warns that Loudness Range should not be confused with other measures such as dynamic range or crest factor.
- `OBS-T3342-002` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3): The computation is based on the **statistical distribution** of measured loudness. Consequences the document states directly: a short but very loud event does not affect the LRA of a longer segment, and the fade-out at the end of a music track does not noticeably increase LRA. The range of the distribution is determined by estimating the difference between a low and a high percentile, a method the document describes as analogous to the Interquartile Range in descriptive statistics.
- `OBS-T3342-003` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3): LRA employs a **cascaded gating** method. The stated rationale is that programmes which are overall very consistent in loudness but contain sections of very low loudness — the document's examples are low-level atmospheres and air-conditioning hum — would otherwise incorrectly receive a high LRA measurement.
- `OBS-T3342-004` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3): **The Loudness Range algorithm is independent of the sample rate and format of the input signal.**
- `OBS-T3342-005` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3.1): The input is a vector of loudness levels computed as specified in ITU-R BS.1770 but with different relative-threshold gating, using a **sliding analysis window of length 3 seconds** for integration. An overlap between consecutive analysis windows **must** be used, to prevent loss of precision on shorter programmes. **A minimum block overlap of 2.9 s between consecutive analysis windows is required — i.e. at least 10 Hz sampling of the loudness level — and the exact amount of overlap is implementation-dependent.** (A 3 s window with ≥2.9 s overlap is a hop of ≤0.1 s, which is the stated ≥10 Hz.)
- `OBS-T3342-006` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3.1): The cascaded gating uses an **absolute threshold of −70 LUFS**, stated to be chosen because no relevant signals are generally found below that loudness level, and a **relative threshold set to −20 LU relative to the absolute-gated loudness level**. The stated purpose of the relative gate is to gate out silence and background sounds by a method independent of any level-normalisation of the input, so that the lower edge of Loudness Range corresponds to the weakest "real" signal rather than to a possibly inaudible noise floor. The stated purpose of the absolute gate is to make the conversion from the relative threshold to an absolute level robust against longer periods of silence or low-level background noise.
- `OBS-T3342-007` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3.1): **LRA is defined as the difference between the estimates of the 10th and the 95th percentiles** of the distribution of the surviving loudness levels. The document gives the reason for each bound: the lower percentile of 10% can prevent the fade-out of a music track from dominating Loudness Range, and the upper percentile of 95% ensures that a single unusually loud sound — its example is a gunshot in a movie — cannot by itself be responsible for a large Loudness Range. The section notes that percentiles are used because the loudness levels cannot in general be assumed to belong to a particular statistical distribution.
- `OBS-T3342-008` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3.1, the worked example accompanying Figure 1): For the film 'The Matrix' (DVD version), the absolute threshold is marked at **−70 LUFS**, the resulting absolute-gated loudness level is **−21.6 LUFS**, the relative threshold sits 20 LU below that at **−41.6 LUFS**, and the resulting Loudness Range is **LRA = 25.0 LU**, taken between the 10th and 95th percentiles of the distribution of loudness levels above the relative threshold. These values are restated in the body text; the plot itself was not machine-readable.
- `OBS-T3342-009` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §3.1): The document warns that measurement of very short programmes where leading or trailing silence is included, or of programmes consisting of isolated utterances, **could result in misleadingly high values of LRA**. This is consistent with `OBS-R128-012`'s recommendation against using LRA below 1 minute.
- `OBS-T3342-010` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §4, Table 1): The LRA minimum-requirement tests, all with a tolerance of **±1 LU** — case 1: two consecutive 20 s in-phase stereo 1 kHz tones at −20.0 and −30.0 dBFS (10 dB apart) expects **LRA = 10 ±1 LU**; case 2: −20.0 and −15.0 dBFS expects **5 ±1 LU**; case 3: −40.0 and −20.0 dBFS expects **20 ±1 LU**; case 4: five 20 s tone segments at −50.0, −35.0, −20.0, −35.0, and −50.0 dBFS expects **15 ±1 LU**; case 5: an authentic narrow-loudness-range stereo programme segment, similar in genre to a commercial or promo, expects **5 ±1 LU**; case 6: an authentic wide-loudness-range stereo segment, similar in genre to a movie or drama, expects **15 ±1 LU**. In all cases the expected response is unchanged if the test signal is repeated at its full length, and the meter shall be reset before each measurement.
- `OBS-T3342-011` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §4): Loudness Range is stated to be **a part of an "EBU Mode" loudness meter** as defined in Tech 3341. Failing the minimum-requirement tests means there is a considerable risk the meter is not compliant with EBU Mode; **passing them does not imply the meter is sufficiently accurate in all respects of its implementation.** Tech 3341 §2.9 makes the identical two-sided statement about its own tests.
- `OBS-T3342-012` **OBSERVED** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3342`, §5, header and framing text only): A reference implementation of the LRA algorithm is provided in the MATLAB language, contributed by TC Electronic, intended to **complement** the textual definition. The document states that other implementations would be equally valid provided the measurements stay within the permitted tolerance, **even though they might yield slightly different LRA measurements for some input signals.** The listing itself was deliberately not extracted; see the coverage note.
- `OBS-T3342-013` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: Case 4 of `OBS-T3342-010` is a gating discriminator worth keeping. Five segments spanning −50.0 to −20.0 dBFS have a 30 dB spread, yet the expected LRA is **15 LU, not 30** — consistent with the −50.0 dBFS segments falling below the −20 LU relative gate and being excluded. An implementation with a broken or absent relative gate would report roughly double. This reading of *why* case 4 produces 15 is an inference; the expected value itself is `OBSERVED`.

## Cross-document candidates

- `OBS-XSTD-001` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: **The two relative gates are different and must not share a code path.** BS.1770 integrated loudness gates 400 ms blocks at 75% overlap with a relative threshold of **−10 LU** (`OBS-BS1770-017`, `OBS-T3341-008`). LRA gates 3 s windows at ≥10 Hz with a relative threshold of **−20 LU** (`OBS-T3342-005`, `OBS-T3342-006`). Only the −70 LUFS absolute threshold is common. Tech 3342 §2 flags this itself with "albeit with a different relative gating threshold". A single shared block pipeline serving both measures would be wrong in both the window length and the threshold, and this is a plausible implementation error that both minimum-requirement suites would catch.
- `OBS-XSTD-002` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: **Momentary is not the same thing as a gating block, despite both being 400 ms.** The BS.1770 gating block is 400 ms with a mandated 75% overlap and is subject to gating (`OBS-BS1770-014`); Momentary Loudness is a 400 ms sliding rectangular window that is explicitly **not gated** and, for live meters, has no stated overlap requirement beyond the general timing tests (`OBS-T3341-004`). The shared 400 ms figure invites conflating them.
- `OBS-XSTD-003` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: **Unit naming is a product decision Spectre has to make explicitly.** BS.1770-5 prints LKFS (`OBS-BS1770-013`); EBU documents print LUFS and state the two are equivalent (`OBS-R128-008`); LU is the relative unit and 1 LU = 1 dB (`OBS-T3341-010`). A Spectre meter must pick a label, and the honest options are to print LUFS with a stated reason, or to print both. What it may not do is imply the two are different quantities.
- `OBS-XSTD-004` **SPECTRE-CANDIDATE**: **"EBU Mode" is a claim with a test suite attached, and Spectre cannot currently make it.** Per `OBS-T3341-001`, EBU Mode is an all-or-nothing activatable mode; per `OBS-T3341-015` it carries mandatory display obligations including the three required measures and the at-most-one-decimal precision; per `OBS-T3341-013`/`-014` it mandates two specific scales with a specific default. Per `OBS-T3341-011`/`OBS-T3342-011`, the compliance evidence is a test-signal set this repository does not have (`GAP-LOUDNESS-0008`). Until those signals are run, Spectre may describe a meter as "measuring integrated loudness per the algorithm of ITU-R BS.1770-5 Annex 1" — a description of what the code does — but must not describe it as "EBU Mode", "R 128 compliant", or "BS.1770 conformant", which are claims about verified behavior.

## Gap register

- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0001` **SOURCE-GAP** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, text after Tables 1 and 2): **BS.1770-5 supplies K-weighting coefficients for 48 kHz only.** It states that other sampling rates require different values chosen to give the same frequency response at 48 kHz, but provides neither those values nor a derivation procedure, nor any tolerance on "the same frequency response". This blocks any Spectre loudness meter at 44.1, 88.2, 96, or 192 kHz from claiming a source-backed coefficient set, and it is the first thing a Spectre metering spec must confront.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0002` **SOURCE-GAP** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 1, equation (6)): The comparison operator in equation (6)'s definition of `J_g` for the **absolute** gating stage did not survive PDF text extraction and could not be read. The general definition preceding equation (5) and equation (7) both use strictly greater-than (`l_j > Γ`). Whether the absolute stage in equation (6) is strictly greater-than or greater-than-or-equal is therefore **unverified from this retrieval**. The distinction is negligible for real signals and non-negligible for a synthesized test tone landing exactly on −70 LKFS. Resolution requires a human reading of the rendered PDF.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0003` **SOURCE-GAP** (all four retrieved documents): **No streaming-platform delivery target exists in any of them, and no named platform appears anywhere.** The only target values retrieved are R 128's −23.0 LUFS and −1 dBTP, both scoped to broadcast programme production (`OBS-R128-002`, `OBS-R128-007`). EBU R 128 s2, "Loudness in Streaming", is cited by R 128 clause r but was **not retrieved**. This gap sits alongside `GAP-OZONE-0005`: neither the vendor corpus nor the standards corpus establishes a streaming target, so nothing in this repository does.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0004` **SOURCE-GAP** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, "recommends" clause 5 and Annex 2): **No single over-sampling factor is mandated.** Annex 2 is titled "Guidelines", recommends clause 5 permits any method giving "similar or superior results", and the 4× figure is the worked example at 48 kHz while the same section states higher ratios are preferred (`OBS-BS1770-022`). A Spectre spec that presents 4× as "the standard's requirement" would misstate the source; the citable facts are the 4×/192 kHz worked example, the "higher is preferred" statement, and the informative under-read table.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0005` **SOURCE-GAP** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annexes 1 and 3): The channel layouts addressed are those of ITU-R BS.775 (up to five main channels) and ITU-R BS.2051 (configurations A–J). **Ordinary stereo is covered only as a subset of Annex 1**, using `G_L = G_R = 1.0` and leaving C/Ls/Rs unused. Layouts outside those two Recommendations — a mono sum, a 2.1 bus, an arbitrary N-channel bus — are not addressed by any retrieved document, and no guidance exists on measuring a bus whose layout is unknown to the meter.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0006` **SOURCE-GAP** (all four documents): **All figures are raster images and were not machine-readable in this retrieval.** This covers BS.1770-5's Figure 1 (algorithm block diagram), Figures 2–4 (stage 1 and stage 2 response curves and the second-order filter signal-flow diagram), and the Annex 2 and Annex 3 block diagrams, plus Tech 3342's Figure 1 distribution plot. No claim in this file derives from a figure. Where a figure is the only statement of a fact — for example the exact signal-flow topology implied by the coefficient naming in Tables 1 and 2 — that fact is **not** recorded as observed.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0007` **SOURCE-GAP** (`SRC-EBU-R128`, Document History versus "recommends" clause h): The Document History table records that V3 (June 2014) changed the Target Level tolerance to **±0.5 LU** except for live programmes, and that V4 (August 2020) made unspecified "Tolerances updates". **No ±0.5 LU figure appears anywhere in the V5 normative text**, which states ±1.0 LU where the target is not practically achievable and ±0.2 LU for workflow measurement error. Whether ±0.5 was superseded, or applied to a case the current text no longer restates, is not resolvable from this document alone. A Spectre spec must cite the V5 figures and must not cite ±0.5 LU.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0008` **SOURCE-GAP** (`SRC-EBU-TECH3341` §2.9; `SRC-EBU-TECH3342` §4): **The EBU minimum-requirement test-signal sets were not retrieved.** Both documents state the signals are available for download from the EBU Technical website; neither embeds them. The *expected responses and tolerances* are recorded above (`OBS-T3341-017`, `-018`, `OBS-T3342-010`) and several cases are synthesizable from their descriptions, but the authentic-programme cases (Tech 3341 cases 7 and 8; Tech 3342 cases 5 and 6) are not. Spectre cannot claim to have passed a suite it has not run.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0009` **SOURCE-GAP** (coverage): Sections not extracted in this pass — Tech 3341 §2.10 "Various interpretation issues", §3, §4, and Table 1 cases beyond 19; Tech 3342 §5 (MATLAB listing, deliberately excluded), §6, §7; BS.1770-5 Attachment 1 to Annex 4, and sections 5 onward of Attachment 1 to Annex 1. Tech 3341 §2.10's title suggests it resolves ambiguities that a first implementation would plausibly hit, and it is the highest-value unread section in the set.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0010` **SOURCE-GAP** (all four documents): **No document specifies where in a signal chain the measurement point sits, nor any realtime implementation constraint.** BS.1770-5 states no required measurement update rate, latency, buffering, or thread model. Tech 3341 states only minimum *display* update rates — ≥10 Hz short-term, ≥1 Hz integrated (`OBS-T3341-004`) — which are display obligations, not DSP scheduling requirements. Every realtime-architecture decision for a Spectre meter is therefore an original Spectre decision governed by `RT-001`–`RT-003`, not a standards inheritance, and needs its own rationale.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0011` **SOURCE-GAP** (cited but not retrieved): **ITU-R BS.1771** (loudness meter requirements — the source of the optional Requirement PLD-4 that Tech 3341 §2.7 references, and of the IIR ballistics that §2.2 declines to adopt) and **Report ITU-R BS.2217** (test material and tolerances for BS.1770 compliance, referenced by both BS.1770-5 NOTE 2 and Tech 3341 §2.9) **have no record in this ledger.** Also cited and not retrieved: EBU R 128 s1–s4, Tech 3343, Tech 3344, Tech 3401, Tech 3205-E, ITU-R BS.645, ITU-R BS.775, ITU-R BS.2051. Note specifically that the "BS.1771" name appearing as an Ozone meter-scale label (`OBS-OZ-METER-005`) refers to a standard **this repository has never read**, which is a second independent reason that label establishes nothing.
- `GAP-LOUDNESS-0012` **SOURCE-GAP** (`SRC-ITU-BS1770-5`, Annex 2): **Annex 2 specifies true-peak estimation for a single channel and states no rule for combining channels.** Whether a programme's Maximum True Peak Level is the maximum across channels, or something else, is not stated in Annex 2. R 128 clause m speaks of "the True Peak Level of a programme" without defining the combination (`OBS-R128-007`), and Tech 3341's true-peak test cases are all stereo signals with identical content in both channels, which cannot discriminate between combination rules (`OBS-T3341-018`). The per-channel maximum is the obvious reading and is **not** recorded as observed.
